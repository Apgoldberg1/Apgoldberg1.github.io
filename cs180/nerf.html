Lego Bulldozer NERF

To create my rays I use my intrinsics matrix to get the ray direction in camera space. Then I transform it to world space with the camera to world matrix (fixing the z to be 1). I then subtract the origin from the resulting point to get the ray direction.

In my dataloader I precompute rays for each pixel and return a random batch of pixel values and rays. In my volume rendering I perform jittered sampling during training by adding a small random offset to the sampling points to avoid overfitting. I use 128 samples per ray for training the lego bulldozer.

I visualized my dataloader by rendering random rays and coloring them based on the pixel value.

I followed the architecture reccomended in the assignement using a width of 256 for my linear layers. I used L=10 for position frequncy encoding and L=4 for view direction frequncy encoding. I did the concatentation of the input into the middle of the network and followed the two branch approach to keep density independent of view direction. To perform volume rendering I used the volume rendering equation and implemented in using torch.cumprod and performed sampling as described above. I was able to train 20 epochs in 35 minutes on a server with a Tesla V100 GPU. I used the Addam optimizer with a learning rate of 5e-2 and a batch size of 8192.

Duck NERF

To achieve the duck NERF, I used a batch size of 16,384, 20 epochs, and a learning rate of 5e-2 with the Adam optimizer. I trained at a resolution of 650x348 and sampled 128 points per ray for training and 256 for inference. I kept the same network architecture as the lego NERF with a width of 256 and L=10 for position and L=4 for view direction. I trained on ~30 images and held out 20% for validation.

To format my images for training I undistort them using cv2.undistort. Then I crop them to enforce cx and cy are centered in the image. I then resize the images and intrinsics appropriately. I used the viser camera frustum visualization to debug this logic and ensure its correctness.
