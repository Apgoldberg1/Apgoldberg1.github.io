Convolution Implementation

The outcome of all three methods is the same, but the double for loop approach improves over the quadruple for loop by vectorizing the application of the convolution filter. I found for the 9x9 box blur the quad for-loop approach took 8.6 seconds, the double for-loop approach took .45 seconds, and scipy's implementation took .0027 seconds. Asymtotically a double vs quad for-loop won't change the runtime since they compute the same number of multiplication and addition operations, but in practice the double for-loop runs much faster on larger filters. When using the finite difference operator however, I found the quadruple for-loop approach was slightly faster which I expect is due to there being more overhead in slicing a numpy array than benefit in vectorizing the filter application when it is only three values. Boundaries are handled by adding padding such that after the convolution is applied the image will be the same size. The image will be reduced in size by the filter dimensions - 1, and my padding accounts for this.

Image Sharpening

Images at different sharpening strengths

The unsharp mask filter works by increasing the relative strength of high frequency features in the image making it appear overall sharper. When we convolve a gaussian kernel on an image we are applying a low-pass filter meaning we remove high frequencies. We can take our original image and subtract it from the low-passsed image to isolate high frequencies. Then we can add the high frequencies back at different strengths in order to "sharpen" our image! This operation can also be combined into one kernel by taking a weighted sum between a unit impulse and negative gaussian kernel.

Images at different sharpening strengths

I was suprised how well the sharpening was able to undo the gaussian blur! The sharpened images look better to me in some places, but with softer elements like trees it starts to look very unnatural for them to have such sharp edges. In the image by 4.0 hill the people in the white flowers in the grass start to pop more as the sharpening increases.

Oraple - Image Blending

"Trivial Merging" refers to directly using a mask to make a binary assignment for which image's pixels to use. Blended mergin utilizes laplacian stacks of both images to obtain a smoothly connected effect!

Gaussian image stacks

Laplacian image stacks

The first 3 rows show masking applied at different laplacian layers while the final row shows the combination of the aggregated laplacian mask. We can see that on the mask seam the image gets blurred with lower frequencies being merged over a larger distance than higher frequencies. The overall result gives the impression of the two images merging smoothly.
